Roles: who runs what

On Sunday a few jobs need doing at once: the screens, the service, the stream and the sound. Roles let a team share those jobs across their computers.

Status: roles arrive in Lampstand 0.8.0. Lampstand is in development and nothing is released yet, so this page describes how roles are designed to work.

Watch: who runs what

What a role is

A role is a job that someone is doing right now. It is not a permission. Each job has one holder at a time, and one computer can hold any mix of jobs. A computer with no job can still plan the service and keep in step with the others.

The four jobs

JobIn plain wordsWhat it looks after
ProjectionRuns the screensThe picture on the projector and the stage screens. It belongs to the computer plugged into them, and it can run on its own with nobody sitting there.
OperatorRuns the serviceNext, back, and jumping to any item. It needs Projection to be running.
StreamRuns what goes onlineOBS, the live stream and its mutes. It keeps working even if the projector computer is away.
AudioRuns the soundThe sound Lampstand plays: videos, music and backing tracks. It plays from the computer next to the mixer.

Only a computer that has screens set up can hold Projection. On any other computer the Take button for Projection is greyed out and says “No screens are set up on this device”.

One computer or several

You can start with one computer and add others later. The service works the same way; each job moves to another computer by asking, except Projection, which stays with the computer wired to the screens.

  • One computer. It holds all four jobs, and there is nothing to set up. OBS and Zoom, if you use them, run on the same computer.
  • Two computers. The one at the projector keeps Projection, Operator and Audio. A second computer, the stream desk, takes Stream and runs OBS and Zoom. Use a wired network if you can.
  • Three computers. A laptop at the back of the room takes Operator, so someone can run the service from where they can see everything. If the projector computer drops off, the Operator waits and wakes when Projection is back, and the stream desk keeps working.
  • Four computers. Where the mixer is far from the projector, a computer at the sound desk takes Audio, so music and videos play straight into the mixer. Projection keeps drawing the screens and stays silent.

Passing a job on

The same four steps work for every job.

  1. Ask. On the computer that wants the job, press Take.
  2. Allow or Not now. The computer holding the job sees who is asking and chooses. If they choose Not now, nothing changes.
  3. Take over. If the holder has been gone for two minutes (the network dropped, or the computer shut), or nobody holds the job, another computer can take it after confirming. You can change the two minutes in Setup.
  4. A computer that comes back learns it lost the job and shows a notice. It does not take the job back by itself.

What happens on the screen: passing Projection clears the screens, and passing Audio stops what is playing, and the question says so before you agree. Passing Operator leaves the slide up, and passing Stream leaves OBS on its current scene.

Phones, tablets and the house

  • Phones and tablets in a browser, such as the tablet remote, are helpers. They send next, back and blank to the computer that runs the screens, but they never hold a job.
  • Stage screens for the band can be a second display on the projector computer, or a web page on a tablet on a music stand.
  • Cameras, microphones and the band do not go through Lampstand. Cameras plug into the stream desk’s OBS, and the microphones come from your mixing desk.

The full design is in the roles guide on GitHub and in decision 0010.
