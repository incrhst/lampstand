# 0012. Recording the sermon: audio in Lampstand, video in OBS, on the Stream holder

- Status: accepted by the owner on 2026-10-25 (what is recorded, which computer, how it starts, the 1.0.0 scope); the details marked **proposed** wait for approval when they are built (tickets T117 to T124)
- Date: 2026-10-25

## Question

Churches want the sermon as a file they can put on their website or podcast the same day. Today they record it in OBS, on a phone held up to a speaker, or not at all. Should Lampstand record the sermon, and if so: what does it record, how does a recording start and stop, which computer records, where do the files go, and how does it fit with OBS?

Two things already decided push against this. Decision [0005](0005-manage-obs-not-replace.md) leaves cameras, encoding and mixing to OBS. The roles spec ([roles.md](../roles.md)) says "Lampstand never carries camera video or sound from the room". So whatever Lampstand records has to stay small, keep OBS in charge of video, and never send room sound anywhere.

## Decision

1. **Lampstand records the sermon's audio itself.** It records from one input chosen in Setup > Recording: usually a feed from the mixer into a USB audio interface, the same feed the stream machine already takes for Zoom and OBS. It records one channel or a stereo pair, at the input's own sample rate, as 24-bit WAV (about 520 MB an hour for one channel). The WAV is the master copy. A church that does not stream can still record. Room sound is still never *carried* anywhere: Lampstand writes it to a file on the computer it came into and sends it nowhere else.
2. **Video stays with OBS.** If the church uses OBS and turns on "Also record video in OBS" (off by default), Lampstand starts OBS's own recording when the sermon recording starts and stops it when the recording stops, through the WebSocket calls it already has (`StartRecord`, `StopRecord`). If OBS was already recording when the sermon started (some churches record the whole service), Lampstand leaves it running and never stops a recording it did not start. It writes down when the sermon started and stopped, so the volunteer knows where to cut. Lampstand does not edit, trim or convert video. That is still a non-goal in the PRD.
3. **The Stream holder records.** Recording is part of the Stream role, "runs the stream" ([decision 0010](0010-roles-and-handoff.md)). It is not a fifth role. The rule follows the OBS duty exactly: the device that holds Stream records; if nobody holds Stream, the Projection holder records; a device with nothing paired records for itself. One machine running everything records with no set-up beyond choosing the input. A church whose mixer feed goes into a laptop at the sound desk lets that laptop take Stream (and connects its OBS, if any, from there).
4. **It starts with the item, or by hand.**
   - An item in the plan can be marked **"Record this"** (normally the sermon). When it goes live, recording starts. When an item that is not marked goes live, recording stops after a 10-second tail, so the last sentence is kept (**proposed**: the length of the tail). If the marked item comes back within the tail (Previous), the same recording carries on. Marked items one after another (the sermon, then a response prayer) make one recording.
   - **Record** and **Stop** in Present work at any time, on any device that runs the service or the screens. A recording started by hand stops only by hand. A recording started by an item stops with the item, or by hand. Stopping by hand always asks first ("Stop recording? The sermon is still on.").
   - Going back to a marked item after the tail starts a new recording, saved as part 2 of the same sermon.
   - A recording stops by itself after 3 hours (**proposed**), so one that was never stopped cannot fill the disk.
5. **Cues travel like scene cues.** When a recording should start or stop, the Projection holder sends a cue to the Stream holder, as it already does for OBS scenes. The newest cue waits if the Stream holder is offline and is acknowledged when it arrives. The Stream holder tells every connected device what it is doing (recording, for how long, the input level, or why it cannot record), and Present shows that on any device. This is sync protocol 10 (two new frames and their acknowledgement). A Stream holder that drops off the network keeps recording, because the recording is local. Its stop cue waits for it, and the 3-hour limit is the backstop.
6. **Passing Stream while recording.** The hand-over prompt says so: "Allow and stop the recording". Passing Stream stops and saves what was recorded. If a marked item is still live, the new holder starts part 2 from its own input. While a recording runs, "Always allow this device" does not apply to Stream: the holder is always asked, so a recording is never cut without someone agreeing. When nothing is recording, passing Stream is unchanged.
7. **Files stay on the computer that recorded them.** They are not synced, because they are large and only one computer needs them. Setup > Recording > "Save recordings in" defaults to a "Lampstand Recordings" folder in the computer's Music folder. A file is named from the service and the item: `2026-11-08 Sunday morning - The good shepherd.wav`, with ` (part 2)` for a second part and characters no file system allows replaced. A small device-local list (`recordings.json`) remembers each recording: the service, the item, when it started, how long it is, the trim points, the exports and any OBS video file. Other devices see "Recordings are on {name}".
8. **A recording survives a crash.** The WAV header is rewritten every five seconds and on stop, so a file cut short by a crash or a power cut plays up to the last few seconds. On the next start Lampstand repairs it and says: "A recording was cut short when Lampstand stopped. It was saved up to 34:12." Recording will not start with less than 2 GB free on the disk, and says why. Present warns when less than 10 minutes of space is left.
9. **Volunteers can see it is working.** Present shows a red "Recording 12:31" pill with a small level meter wherever recording matters. If almost no sound comes in for 30 seconds, it says "No sound is coming in to the recording. Check the feed from the mixer." The role bar warns ahead of a marked item when it cannot be recorded, naming the reason: "The sermon won't be recorded: no input is chosen on Sanctuary." The microphone permission (macOS, Windows) is asked in Setup > Recording when the input is chosen, never in the middle of a service.
10. **Trimming and export.** A Recordings page (in Plan, on the device that holds the recordings) lists them, newest first. For each one: a waveform, play from any point, and two handles to trim the start and the end. A trim is a pair of points; the master WAV never changes. Cutting out the middle is a later feature. **Export** writes an MP3 (mono 96 kbps, stereo 128 kbps), with tags from the plan: title from the item, album from the service, artist from the church name, and the date. **"Even out the level"** (on by default) brings the loudness to the usual podcast level. Export can also write a trimmed WAV. Exports go beside the master. "Show in folder" opens the folder. **Move to Trash** removes a recording (the system's Trash, not a permanent delete). Uploading to a website or podcast host is a later feature.
11. **MP3 encoding uses LAME** (LGPL, through a Rust wrapper), because no pure-Rust MP3 encoder is mature. It is built from source into the app, so nothing is installed beside it. `deny.toml` allows `LGPL-2.0-or-later` for that crate only, as the root certificates are allowed today. **Proposed:** if the wrapper turns out to be a poor fit on one of the three systems, export FLAC as well (pure Rust) and ship MP3 when it works.

## What this changes in existing docs

- **[roles.md](../roles.md):** Stream also owns the recording. The sentence "Lampstand never carries camera video or sound from the room" gains: "It records the sermon's audio from one input, on the computer that runs the stream, and sends it nowhere." The people-facing words for Stream stay "runs the stream" (**proposed**); Setup > Devices adds "and records the sermon" under it.
- **[PRD](../PRD.md):** a Recording section (RC-1 to RC-5). The non-goal "Video editing" stands.
- **T92** (stream and record from the service order) keeps starting the *stream* from an item. Recording video from the order is covered here instead.

## Options considered

- **What is recorded.** Audio only, with no OBS link, leaves streaming churches recording video by hand. Audio and video both in Lampstand would reverse decision 0005 and add capture devices, audio-video sync and encoders for each system: months of work, and an extra load on the computer that may also drive the projectors. Chosen: audio here, video in OBS.
- **Which computer.** A fifth role, Recording, would let any computer record whatever holds Stream, but it adds a role to the roster and to every hand-over rule for one job. Making it part of Audio puts it on the computer that *plays* sound, which is often not the one that *takes in* the mixer feed. A per-computer switch allows two backups but shows nothing on the roster, so nobody knows who is recording. Chosen: part of Stream, because the stream machine is where the documented mixer feed already goes, and the OBS duty already falls back the same way.
- **Starting.** By hand only is easy to forget. Starting with the item and stopping only by hand loses the stop if the volunteer forgets. Chosen: with the item, and by hand.
- **Recording through OBS only** (Lampstand starts and stops OBS's recording and does nothing else). It is cheap, but churches without OBS get nothing, the file is a video, and trimming and MP3 export would need a video toolchain.

## Risks

- **Input devices behave differently on each system.** Shared access to a USB interface (Lampstand recording while OBS and Zoom read the same input) works in shared mode on macOS and Windows, but has not been tested here. Linux runs in CI only. A real-hardware check before release covers it.
- **The Stream holder is often the computer busiest with OBS.** A WAV writer is light (no encoding while recording), but T61 measures it beside OBS on the 2019 laptop.
- **People being recorded.** The church decides what to record and publish. Lampstand records only what is marked or started by hand, and the pill shows whenever it is recording.

## Not decided here

- Cutting out the middle of a recording, chapters, transcripts, and uploading to a podcast host or website.
- Recording music or the whole service as audio (it works by hand; nothing in the plan is built around it).
- A browser view of the recording status for a phone.
